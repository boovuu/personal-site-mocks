Concept

1 of 8

A coin carries value, has two faces, and can be tossed. Here the toss stands for chance: it offers a starting condition, never a verdict. One face holds a compass, for ambition given a direction. The other holds Cassiopeia, a real northern-sky landmark whose five-star shape can help locate Polaris. The wider universe is your poetic frame for awareness beyond the immediate. In DICE, chance supplies a seed; the maker decides what it means and what to make from it.

“Reckoning” names the work after the toss: taking bearings from what is known, choosing a course, then correcting it as new information appears. “The Turning Coin” remains a possible, more literal title if you want motion rather than judgment to lead.

The coin

Value · two faces · chance

Brass carries some of gold’s associations with value and durability. The toss introduces randomness, but it does not decide what the work means.

The compass

Direction · ambition · voyage

A bearing is a chosen direction, not a promise of arrival. It gives the personal identity a sense of intent and forward movement.

Cassiopeia

Orientation · perspective · cosmos

Its familiar W shape helps point toward Polaris. The larger-consciousness meaning belongs to your personal symbolism, not to an astronomical claim.

Historical grounding: the Met catalogs a Roman bronze coin dated 225–217 BCE with Janus on one face and a ship’s prow on the other. NASA’s guide describes Cassiopeia as one of the constellations used to find the Pole Star; Royal Museums Greenwich describes Polaris as a navigational reference and the mariner’s astrolabe as a way to measure celestial altitude for latitude. These are visual and navigational precedents, not evidence that this coin was historically tossed to decide outcomes. Obverse

Bearing

Compass rose, eight points. The course line runs to 022.5° (NNE), not to north. Direction, ambition, and the nerve to set out. The personal site leads with this face.

Reverse

Seed

Cassiopeia, drawn from real star positions, with a dashed line toward Polaris. It gives a point of orientation; the Bearing face chooses where to go. DICE leads with this face. Same coin, other side up.

Small mark: Needle & Star

3 of 8

Below 64 px the coin is reduced to three parts: the rim, the needle from the Bearing face, and one star from the Seed face. The needle is set at 022.5°, so even at favicon size it points somewhere chosen rather than due north. DICE gets a sibling that keeps the five stars.

  1. Drawn on a 32-unit grid, so the 16 px favicon lands every edge on a half pixel at worst.
  2. Rim stroke is 2.1 units: thick enough to survive 16 px, thin enough that the ring reads as a coin edge, not a button.
  3. Needle and star share one axis, rotated 22.5° (one compass point) clockwise from north.
  4. Gap between star and rim is never under 1.5 units. That gap is what keeps the star separate at small sizes.
  5. Rust on the star only from 48 px up. Smaller, the whole mark is one colour.

Linework, texture, framing

Linework

Four weights only, as a share of mark diameter: rim 1.5%, rule 0.5%, course dash 0.55%, hairline 0.3%. Never under 1 px rendered. No cross-hatching below 96 px.

Texture

Three, used one at a time. Paper grain at 4% on cream grounds. A brass graticule on teal for DICE charts and maps. A milled-edge rule, taken from the coin’s edge, as a section divider.

Register · No. 0417Entry framed with a single rule, title set into the top edge, bearing and date into the bottom.022.5° · 11.10.26

Framing

The register frame. One rule, labels let into the line like a ledger. Used for seeds, quotes and evidence, not for every card.

Image direction

Use
  • Real materials in side light: paper, brass, wood, the actual objects a seed named.
  • Single-colour line drawings at the four stroke weights, one figure per image.
  • Charts, maps and process diagrams drawn as instruments: ticks, scales, true units.
  • Work shown as evidence. Screens, drafts and before/after at true size.
Avoid
  • Nebula photography, lens flare, glowing particles, starfields as backgrounds.
  • Ships, anchors, skulls, flags, crowns, rope borders.
  • Zodiac wheels or anything that reads as fortune telling.
  • Gradient meshes, glassy 3D coins, crypto-style metallic renders.

About the idea

DICE starts with chance: an object, a condition, a limit. The seed does not say what the work means. I read it, choose a direction, and make something from it. The coin carries that method: value, two faces, and a random landing. The compass stands for ambition with a bearing. Cassiopeia connects navigation to the larger sky; the idea of wider awareness is personal symbolism. Chance starts the piece. The maker carries it forward.
