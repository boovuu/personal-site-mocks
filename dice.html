Why the draw is deterministic

Most randomness is a black box: you cannot check what the machine almost said. D.I.C.E. is the opposite. The five words are normalized and hashed with SHA-256; the hash bytes select the cards and their orientation. Identical input yields an identical draw, byte for byte, on any machine that runs the rules. Nothing is simulated and nothing is remembered between runs: the draw is pure arithmetic on your words.

The lineage is short and public. Edward de Bono's random entry (1970) showed that a deliberately arbitrary input breaks a stuck line of thought. Brian Eno's Oblique Strategies (1975) put the interruption on cards. In 2026, thirty-four field sessions turned the idea into an instrument with laws, and the laws into an engine. What Eno left to chance, this engine pins to a hash.

The seed protocol

You give five arbitrary words, in any language, in any register. The engine never reads them for meaning. It lowercases them, strips diacritics and everything that is not a letter or digit, and hashes what survives. The words do one job: they select the cards. None of them, and nothing they evoke, may surface in a reading. That is the invisible-spice rule, and it is why the same brief with different words produces a different deal instead of a different decoration.

The deck

The canon is the full 78-card Rider–Waite deck: 22 majors and 56 minors, never swapped or reskinned. It is used as infrastructure, not as fortune-telling. Three translation layers sit on top: each major becomes a systems archetype (the Tower, a structural reset; the Star, a calibration beacon); each suit becomes an operational domain (Wands, drive and conflict; Cups, bonds and intuition; Swords, mind and cost; Pentacles, money, body, and time); each rank becomes a weight, from the Ace's raw spark to the Ten's terminal load, with the court cards as roles: executor, accelerator, strategist, commander.

Each position in a spread has a class: state (what is), pressure (what blocks), engine (what drives), vector (what moves). A card's meaning bends to its position; the Tower in a state slot describes standing instability, in a vector slot it prescribes the teardown.

The entropy index

The last byte of the hash becomes a number from 0 to 9, the radicalism budget. Zero to two: refine the current direction surgically. Three to six: a strong pivot; change the spine, keep the skeleton. Seven to nine: full teardown, nothing survives by seniority. It is a heuristic, a budget for how hard to swing, and it is stated in every manifest so the advice and the number can be checked against each other.

The clarifier

When the base spread is not enough, three more cards come from the remaining undealt cards, continuing the same hash stream: the Knot (what is actually tangled), the Hidden Hand (the unseen force), and the Way Through (the exit the base spread could not see). One clarifier per reading. Still unresolved after that, the problem is the brief, not the cards: re-brief and re-seed.

The laws, distilled

  • The seed is law. The draw obeys the stated seed and constraints, or the reading is void.
  • Radical divergence. Consecutive readings must differ in substance. A re-skin of yesterday's reading is a failed draw.
  • Story, not keywords. The engine deals raw material; the reader shapes it into the situation. Delete the card names and the text must still read as sharp strategy.
  • The deck is canon. 78 cards, three translation layers, no substitutions. Depth comes from the clarifier, never from a new deck.
  • The invisible spice. The seed words are spent the moment the cards are dealt. If you can find your words in the reading, the reader failed.
  • The machinery shows its work. Every manifest carries the hash, the entropy index, and the directive, so any claim can be walked back to the draw that produced it.

What it is not

Not a fortune teller: nothing here predicts outcomes, and no claim on this page depends on it doing so. Not a yes/no machine: it refuses questions a coin flip could answer. Not a substitute for professional help on medical, legal, or psychological crises. And not a party trick: the output is only as good as the question fed to it, which is the point.

Colophon

This page runs a JavaScript port of the reference engine (engine.py v0.3, Python). The port is verified against the original by a parity script that runs both engines on the same seeds, including diacritics, mixed case, and a pure-Cyrillic seed, and compares every card, orientation, meaning, and byte of the printed manifest: 7 of 7 identical, verified 15 October 2026. The script lives in the site's repository at scripts/verify-dice-port.js.

Scope limits: what runs here is the deal, not the reading. The interpretation layer is a separate craft, exercised privately; the reading logbook is not published. The entropy index is a heuristic budget, not a measurement of your situation, and the draw constrains nothing: it opens a starting condition and the judgement stays yours.
