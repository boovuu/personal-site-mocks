WOS: carrying the work beyond launch

I could see what had shipped. Understanding what happened afterwards meant opening several tools and recovering the context. WOS grew out of the work needed to connect those two moments.

The website still needs an owner after launch.

My responsibility for the website includes campaign and product pages, reusable Webflow templates, forms, measurement and consent implementation. Marketing, design, operations and security each bring decisions that have to reach the finished page.

That work continues after publication. Is the page available to search engines? Has its content reached the Knowledge Hub? Can someone find it? What does the traffic tell us? Answering those questions repeatedly exposed the same gaps between systems.

I built WOS as a workspace around those jobs. The starting point was the work people needed to carry through.

A publishing button carries several decisions.

A request to include content in the Knowledge Hub looked small on the surface. The control needed to know which content qualified, where its image came from, which date and format to use, how topics and industries connected, and what should happen if an item was already present.

I turned those decisions into content rules, an editing workflow and checks around the operation. Blog posts and customer stories could have different source fields while contributing to a common destination.

I built search over titles, descriptions and the text inside each resource, including related terms. A reader does not have to know the exact title to reach the material. For the team, it makes the content index and its relationships part of the publishing work: the question is also whether someone can retrieve what we have published.

  1. Eligibility. Define which source collections can contribute and which publishing event should trigger a change.
  2. Metadata and imagery. Carry the title, publication date, format and cover image, with an explicit fallback where needed.
  3. Relationships. Keep industry and topic references connected to the right collections.
  4. Duplication. Check whether the destination item exists before creating another copy.
  5. Control. Make inclusion, exclusion and the result of the operation visible to the person doing the work.

The content index is a stored copy of the Knowledge Hub pages. Keeping it current is another responsibility to carry through after publication.

Each connection needs a job.

I built integrations around specific questions and operations. Some read evidence from another system. Others move content or respond to a publishing event. Keeping those roles clear matters when a team needs to understand what a control will change.

This gives the team a way to trace a question back to its source. A traffic question belongs with GA4; an indexing question needs the search-engine response; a missing careers listing leads to the hiring sync. I keep the dependencies and limits visible so someone can decide where to investigate.

Webflow
Pages, CMS items, assets and publishing operations, with write controls governed by roles and configuration.
GA4
Traffic and behaviour data associated with the live site and individual pages.
Google Search Console + Bing
Search and indexing evidence, kept alongside live-page checks so conflicting signals can be inspected.
Microsoft Clarity
Behaviour signals, including recorded rage and dead clicks that can inform a page review.
AirOps + Profound
AI-answer visibility and citation data, connected to page and topic investigations.
Greenhouse
Job-board data connected to the careers CMS through webhook handling and reconciliation workflows.
Slack + GitHub
Feedback, activity reporting, change history and scheduled checks.

HubSpot forms and OneTrust consent are also part of my public-site responsibilities. They have their own role in the visitor journey; they are separate from the WOS integration catalogue.

Carry the page’s context into the review.

I developed Smart Indexing and Launch Report work around the evidence those connections make available. A page may be published, present in a sitemap and still have a different indexing verdict. Those observations need to remain distinguishable.

The indexing work brings the page, live checks and search-engine responses into one review. The launch-report work brings together available traffic, search, behaviour and citation signals around a page over time.

I grouped launches by the attention they need and made the next step part of the report. In this example, one page needs a meta description, another has a noindex conflict, and a customer story is missing from the Knowledge Hub. Those are different jobs for the team to review.

The checkpoints also preserve the timing. A new page may be ready for a release check while it is still too early to judge its traffic. The report gives the person reviewing it that context before they decide what to change.

A report is useful when it helps someone decide what to inspect or change. Missing data needs to remain visible, and a change in traffic needs context before it becomes a claim about the effect of a release.

Build the connections and the operating rules.

I built the application features and integrations while continuing the website work. That includes the content relationships, sync behaviour, checks, permissions and feedback that make a workflow understandable to the next person using it.

The wider team supplies the commercial priorities, content, design and specialist decisions. My work is to carry those decisions into an implementation, expose the dependencies and preserve enough context for the team to continue.

Use the context to choose a relevant response.

The developing account-based marketing work asks a related question: once we can inspect a company’s path through the site, which content or follow-up would actually help?

I am exploring landing pages organised around an industry, a problem or a solution, then content offers that carry the visitor’s earlier question forward. The Atlas demonstration follows a returning account through a personalised offer and into the context sales needs to choose its next response.

This is a developing workflow with a fictional account demonstration. A client pilot and measured commercial results would be the next evidence to add.
