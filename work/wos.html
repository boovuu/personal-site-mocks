WOS: a workspace for the website team

I built WOS to bring content operations, search visibility and page performance into one workspace. The team can find the right material, investigate a page and see what needs attention after launch.

My role
Website delivery, custom application work and integrations within an agency and client team.
The setting
An enterprise marketing site with campaigns, a Knowledge Hub, analytics, consent and hiring workflows.
The work
Knowledge Hub search, integrations and page-level reporting with proposed next steps.

Keep the website useful as the work grows.

I work across campaign and product pages, reusable Webflow templates, forms, measurement and consent. Within the wider agency and client team, I carry decisions from marketing, design, operations and security into the live website.

That responsibility also means making the existing site work better. Content needs to be findable. New pages need to reach search engines. The team needs enough context to decide where to spend its time.

WOS brings those jobs together. I built the application features and integrations around the content and pages the team already works with. These three views show the work: a searchable Knowledge Hub, the systems connected around it and a Launch Report that turns page checks into follow-up.

Make the content library easier to use.

I built Knowledge Hub search across resource titles, descriptions and article text, including related terms. Someone looking for an answer can find relevant material without knowing the title or the language used in the headline.

Blog posts and customer stories feed a shared library, organised by format, industry and topic. The view below separates title matches from matches inside an article, so the reader can see why a resource appeared.

Behind the search, I built the content rules and relationships that keep the library consistent. Different source collections can contribute their own fields while retaining a common structure for titles, images, formats and topics. Existing entries are checked before new ones are created.

For the content team, this makes maintaining the library part of the website workflow. They can manage which resources appear and how they are grouped. The search index also needs to stay current as that content changes.

Connect the tools around the page.

I connected WOS to the systems the team uses to publish content, understand performance and maintain the site. Webflow supplies the pages and CMS records. Analytics, search engines and behaviour tools add evidence about what happens to them.

The practical value is in following a question across those systems. A page can be live in Webflow while Google reports an indexing problem. Traffic can look healthy while Clarity points to a broken interaction. I bring those signals into the page review so the team has context for its next decision.

Webflow
Pages, CMS items, assets and publishing operations, with write controls governed by roles and configuration.
GA4
Traffic and behaviour data associated with the live site and individual pages.

My wider website work also includes HubSpot forms and OneTrust consent, connecting lead capture and measurement to the visitor journey.

Give each launch a next step.

I developed Launch Report to give the team a page-level view of what needs attention after release. It brings together available traffic, search, behaviour and citation signals, with checkpoints and a proposed next step for each page.

The related Smart Indexing work brings live-page checks and search-engine responses into the same review. A page being published, appearing in a sitemap and being indexed are separate observations. Keeping them visible helps the team locate the problem.

I grouped launches by the attention they need. In this example, one page needs a meta description, another has a noindex conflict, and a customer story is missing from the Knowledge Hub. The team can move from the overview to a specific piece of work, with the evidence still attached.

The checkpoints also preserve the timing. A new page may be ready for a release check while it is still too early to judge its traffic. The report gives the person reviewing it that context before they decide what to change.

I keep missing data visible and leave the decision with the team. A traffic change can prompt an investigation; understanding the effect of a release takes more context.

What I took responsibility for.

I built the application features and integrations alongside ongoing website delivery. My work covers the search and content relationships, data connections, sync behaviour and checks that support these views, as well as the controls and feedback the team needs to use them.

The wider team supplies the commercial priorities, content, design and specialist decisions. My work is to carry those decisions into an implementation, expose the dependencies and preserve enough context for the team to continue.
